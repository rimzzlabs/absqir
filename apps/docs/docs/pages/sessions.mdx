# Sessions and check-in

A session is one moment people are expected: a shift, a meeting, a
workshop. It has a start, an end, a late threshold, and one or more groups.
Everyone in those groups is on the list.

## The session clock

A session moves through three statuses on its own. Nothing is stored; the
status is read from the timestamps every time.

| Status      | When                                                                                                                |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `scheduled` | Before check-in opens.                                                                                              |
| `running`   | From `opens before` minutes ahead of the start until the end, or from the moment an organizer presses **Open now**. |
| `done`      | At the end, or when an organizer presses **Close now**.                                                             |

When a session becomes done, every expected person without a record gets an
`absent` record. That happens on the next read after the end, or at once on
a manual close, so no cron job is required.

A session can be created with times that already passed, to record something
that happened before it was written. It closes on the next read and marks
everyone expected absent, so fix the records afterwards. The dialog warns
before you save, and the organizers get no closed notification for it.

## Statuses per person

| Status    | How it happens                                                               |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `present` | Checked in no later than `late after` minutes past the start.                |
| `late`    | Checked in after that, while the session still runs.                         |
| `excused` | Set by an organizer by hand, or written when a leave request is approved.    |
| `absent`  | Written when the session closes, for everyone expected who never checked in. |

An organizer can change any status by hand from the session page. The record
remembers how it came to be: `screen`, `scanner`, `manual`, or `auto`.

## Two ways to check in

**The room screen.** The organizer opens the session's room screen on a
projector or a tablet. It shows a QR code that encodes a link with a token.
The token is an HMAC over the session id and the current 20 second window,
keyed by a per-session secret that never leaves the server, so a photo of
the code expires within seconds. A member scans it from **Check in** in the
app, which opens the camera, or with the phone camera, which opens the link
and signs the member in if the phone asks. Only a signed-in member of the
organization can use the link, and only if they are on the list, unless the
session allows walk-ins.

**The scanner at the door.** Each member has a pass for the session on
**Check in** and on **My sessions**: a QR code signed with the session
secret and bound to that person. The organizer opens the scanner page,
points the camera at the pass, and the person is in. A pass can be pasted
as text when the camera is not available.

Either way, the clock decides present or late. A second scan of the same
person changes nothing and says so.

## Schedules

A schedule is a rule: every weekday at 09:00 for 60 minutes, or every
Tuesday at 19:00. It carries its own late threshold, opening margin, groups,
and timezone, and spawns sessions two weeks ahead. The sessions appear when
anyone lists them, so a fresh rule shows its first sessions at once. Editing
a rule replaces the future sessions nobody has touched; the ones that ran
stay as they were.

## Public registration

A session can open a public page. Tick **Open a public page where anyone
can register** on the session, set a seat limit or leave it empty, and copy
the link from the session page. The link looks like `/e/<session id>`.

The page works signed out. It shows the title, the times, the organization,
and how many seats are taken. A visitor who registers:

- signs in, or creates an account with a code sent to their email,
- joins the organization as a `member` if they are not one yet, with a
  directory row,
- lands on the session's list, so the session expects them like anyone in
  a group.

A stranger can create an account through this page even when
`REGISTRATION_OPEN` is off. The page sets a short-lived cookie with the
session id, and the sign-up door accepts a new email only while that cookie
names a session that still takes people. A session takes people until it
ends, or until an organizer unticks the box.

A registered person can withdraw before the session opens. On the session
page, registered people carry a **Registered** label. The expected count
and the absent records at close include them.

## Leave requests

A member who cannot make a session asks for leave on **My leave** before
the session ends: pick the session, give a reason, send. One request per
session. A pending request can be withdrawn.

Organizers, admins, and owners see the queue on **Leave requests**. An
approval writes an `excused` record for that session with the note, so the
close does not mark the person absent. A decline leaves the record as it
is. Either way, the member sees the decision and the note on **My leave**.
A decided request cannot be changed; an organizer who changes their mind
sets the status by hand on the session page.

## For members

**Check in** is the way in: the camera reads the room screen, and the
sessions that run right now show a pass for the door. **My sessions**
lists what expects you, from yesterday on, whether through
a group or a registration, with your record once you have one and your
pass while the session runs. **History** lists every closed session with
your status and an attendance rate. Nobody else in the organization sees
your history page.

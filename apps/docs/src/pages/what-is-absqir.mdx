# What absqir is

<div role="doc-subtitle">The model, the words, and the things absqir does not do</div>

absqir records who turned up. An organizer puts a QR code on a screen. The
people in the room scan it with their own phones. The code changes every 20
seconds, so a photograph of it is dead before it travels.

You run absqir yourself, on your own machine, against your own Postgres.

Read this page first. It takes two minutes, and it gives you the six words the
rest of the documentation uses.

## The model

<HowAbsqirWorks />

| Word             | What it is                                                                            |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Organization** | Everything belongs to one. An account can join several, with a different role in each. |
| **Person**       | One row in the directory. A person can exist before they have an account.              |
| **Group**        | A set of people: a team, a division, a class, a cohort.                                |
| **Event**        | One moment people are expected. It has a start, an end, and one or more groups.        |
| **Record**       | One row per person per event: present, late, excused, or absent.                       |
| **Report**       | The records of many events, counted over a range of days.                              |

The chain runs one way. The groups say who is expected. The event says when.
Check-in writes a record for each person who arrives. The close writes an
absent record for everyone else. Reports count the records.

## Who runs it

- An office that must record shifts, and pay or roster from the result.
- A school, a course, or a bootcamp that takes attendance in a room.
- A community that runs meetups, and wants to know who came back.

Each of these is one organization. One instance can hold many.

## What absqir does not do

- It does not do payroll, and it does not calculate pay.
- It does not read location, and it does not watch people between events.
- It does not have a hosted version yet. Today you self-host it.

## Two words that mean the same thing

The app says **event**. The API, the database, and the addresses still say
`event`: `/events`, `/my/events`, `event`. Only the copy
changed, because a signed-in browser is also an event, and one word for two
things confused people. Nothing you link or script breaks.
